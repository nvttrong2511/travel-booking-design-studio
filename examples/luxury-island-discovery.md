# Worked Example — Luxury Island Discovery

## Problem
Create a premium island discovery experience for travelers who value privacy, atmosphere, and confidence more than bargain comparison.

## Emotional target
Feel like entering a private atlas curated around personal rhythm rather than browsing a catalogue.

## Direction A — Tidal Atlas
A map-first canvas where destinations expand and contract like tides according to season, travel time, and desired energy. The signature interaction is a route scrubber connecting mood, weather, and transfer complexity. Mobile becomes a focused vertical journey with one active island at a time.

## Direction B — Quiet Chapters
An editorial sequence organized as chapters: arrival, solitude, water, night, and return. Inventory appears inside narrative moments rather than in a uniform card grid. Mobile preserves chapter pacing with compact booking facts pinned beneath the active story.

## Direction C — Horizon Rooms
A spatial comparison theater where each candidate destination occupies a horizon layer. Dragging between layers changes soundless motion, climate tone, travel effort, and price context. Mobile converts the theater into a swipeable decision stack with a persistent comparison rail.

## Critic verdict
Tidal Atlas is the most original but risks map complexity. Quiet Chapters is emotionally strongest but may slow direct comparison. Horizon Rooms best balances distinction and booking clarity.

## Selected direction
Horizon Rooms, using the editorial pacing of Quiet Chapters for destination detail pages.

## Similarity warning
Avoid conventional destination cards inside the horizon layers. Each layer must remain a spatial state with clear price, dates, transfer effort, and availability.
